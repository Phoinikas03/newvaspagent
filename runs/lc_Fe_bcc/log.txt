functionals\n=======================================================================================================================================\n\n[1 Reply](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/2020/02/18/optimal-lattice-parameter-determination-of-fe-bcc-structure-using-the-generalized-gradient-approximations-with-pbe-and-pw91-functionals/#comments)\n\n**Introduction**\n================\n\nIron\xa0(Fe) is known to have a bcc crystal structure at temperatures lower than\xa01100 K [1]. Its optimal lattice parameters\xa0for\xa0this\xa0structure\xa0are derived using Density-Functional Theory (DFT) methods. The Cambridge Serial Total Energy Package (CASTEP) software package, which uses plane-wave basis sets in order to analyze crystal structures, is implemented to analyze the\xa0bcc\xa0lattices of\xa0Fe\xa0to determine the optimal parameters.\xa0Two functionals in\xa0the generalized gradient\xa0approximations, PBE and PW91, are used to derive these parameters. The PBE and PW91 functionals are\xa0expected to produce virtually identical results [2].\xa0A comparison between the results obtained from these functionals\xa0is made along with the computational costs associated with both of them.\xa0\xa0\n\nMethodology\n===========\n\nWe use the plane wave DFT calculations to evaluate the\xa0optimal lattice parameter for Fe bcc crystal structure.\xa0All the calculations were performed using the CASTEP Simulation Package\xa0in Material Studio. The exchange and correlation energies were calculated using the\xa0PBE\xa0and the PW 91 functional described within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). Electronic convergence tolerance of 2E-06 was used for all the calculations. The core electrons were treated using “on the fly” generated (OTFG) ultra-soft pseudo-potential with a core radius of 2.4 Bohrs (1.27 Angstrom) generated with a panel of 8 valence electrons ( 3d6 4s2).\xa0All the calculations are performed with the spin-polarized option ON as Fe is a magnetic element.\xa0\n\nCut-off Energy and K-Point Optimization\n---------------------------------------\n\nThe essential step before performing a plane-wave basis set is to optimize the k-points and cut-off energy. This process is performed with both the functionals.\xa0\n\n### K-point\n\n[![](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/K_PW91-300x225.jpeg)](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/K_PW91.jpeg)\n\nFig.1 K-point convergence for PW91 functional. The Y-axis is the energy difference between successive calculations. The red line represents the convergence criteria.\n\n[![](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/K_PBE-300x225.jpeg)](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/K_PBE.jpeg)\n\nFig. 2 K-point convergence for PBE functional. The Y-axis is the energy difference between successive calculations. The red line represents the convergence criteria.\n\nThe experimentally determined optimal lattice parameter for Fe bcc crystal structure is 2.856 Angstrom [1]. However, it is more appropriate to perform k-point optimization on the lowest lattice dimension being investigated. As the smallest lattice parameter in real space gives the longest parameter in k-space. Hence the K-point optimization is performed at the lattice parameter of 2.3 Angstrom. A very high cut-off energy value is selected here 500 eV to avoid its effects on k-point optimization.\n\nTable 1: K-point grid vs the number of K-points used\n----------------------------------------------------\n\n| K-point grid | K-points used |\n| --- | --- |\n| 3x3x3 | 4 |\n| 4x4x4 | 6 |\n| 5x5x5 | 10 |\n| 6x6x6 | 14 |\n| 7x7x7 | 20 |\n| 8x8x8 | 26 |\n| 9x9x9 | 35 |\n| 10x10x10 | 44 |\n| 12x12x12 | 68 |\n| 15x15x15 | 120 |\n\nFigs.1 and 2 show the absolute change in the consecutive total energy (|ΔE|)\xa0with respect to\xa0the number of irreducible k-points. The corresponding K-point grid is shown in Table 1. As a convergence criterion\xa0of |ΔE| less than 0.04\xa0eV\xa0is used for both the functionals. This corresponds to the optimal grid of 9x9x9.\xa0\n\n### Cut-off energy\n\n[![](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/cut_PW91-300x225.jpeg)](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/cut_PW91.jpeg)\n\nFig. 3 Cut-off energy convergence for PW91 functional. The Y-axis is the energy difference between successive calculations. The red line represents the convergence criteria.\n\n[![](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/cut_PBE-300x225.jpeg)](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/cut_PBE.jpeg)\n\nFig. 4 Cut-off energy convergence for PBE functional. The Y-axis is the energy difference between successive calculations. The red line represents the convergence criteria.\n\nIn Figs. 3 and 4, the absolute change in the consecutive total energy (|ΔE|)\xa0is plotted as a function of\xa0the cut-off energy. As a convergence criterion,\xa0it is\xa0assumed\xa0that the total energy is converged if |ΔE| is less\xa0than 0.03 eV. From Figs. 3 and 4,\xa0it is clear\xa0that the cut-off energy of\xa0400 eV sufficient\xa0considering both the functionals.\xa0A lattice parameter of 2.856 Angstrom and K-point grid of 9x9x9 is used for this convergence.\xa0\n\nFe lattice optimization\n-----------------------\n\nTo optimize the Fe bcc lattice parameter,\xa0the crystal structure energy is calculated for the bcc geometry while changing the lattice parameters from 2.3 to 3.5 angstrom with an interval of 0.1 Angstrom. Based on the K-point and cut off energy convergence study, cut-off energy of 400 eV and a K-point grid of 9x9x9 is employed in these calculations.\xa0\xa0\n\nResults\n=======\n\n[![](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/opt-4-300x225.jpeg)](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/opt-4.jpeg)\n\nFig. 5 shows the lattice parameter\xa0optimization. A curve is fitted to the DFT data to get an estimate of the energy minima with respect to the lattice parameter. A polynomial fit of order 4 is used for this purpose for data ranging from lattice parameter 2.6 to 3. Table 2 compares the optimum lattice parameters calculated using both the functionals. Both the functionals give optimum lattice parameter values close to the experimental data. There is hardly any difference between the values determined by both functionals. the generalized gradient approximations with PBE and PW91 functionals\n=======================================================================================================================================\n\n[1 Reply](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/2020/02/18/optimal-lattice-parameter-determination-of-fe-bcc-structure-using-the-generalized-gradient-approximations-with-pbe-and-pw91-functionals/#comments)\n\n**Introduction**\n================\n\nIron\xa0(Fe) is known to have a bcc crystal structure at temperatures lower than\xa01100 K [1]. Its optimal lattice parameters\xa0for\xa0this\xa0structure\xa0are derived using Density-Functional Theory (DFT) methods. The Cambridge Serial Total Energy Package (CASTEP) software package, which uses plane-wave basis sets in order to analyze crystal structures, is implemented to analyze the\xa0bcc\xa0lattices of\xa0Fe\xa0to determine the optimal parameters.\xa0Two functionals in\xa0the generalized gradient\xa0approximations, PBE and PW91, are used to derive these parameters. The PBE and PW91 functionals are\xa0expected to produce virtually identical results [2].\xa0A comparison between the results obtained from these functionals\xa0is made along with the computational costs associated with both of them.\xa0\xa0\n\nMethodology\n===========\n\nWe use the plane wave DFT calculations to evaluate the\xa0optimal lattice parameter for Fe bcc crystal structure.\xa0All the calculations were performed using the CASTEP Simulation Package\xa0in Material Studio. The exchange and correlation energies were calculated using the\xa0PBE\xa0and the PW 91 functional described within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). Electronic convergence tolerance of 2E-06 was used for all the calculations. The core electrons were treated using “on the fly” generated (OTFG) ultra-soft pseudo-potential with a core radius of 2.4 Bohrs (1.27 Angstrom) generated with a panel of 8 valence electrons ( 3d6 4s2).\xa0All the calculations are performed with the spin-polarized option ON as Fe is a magnetic element.\xa0\n\nCut-off Energy and K-Point Optimization\n---------------------------------------\n\nThe essential step before performing a plane-wave basis set is to optimize the k-points and cut-off energy. This process is performed with both the functionals.\xa0\n\n### K-point\n\n[![](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/K_PW91-300x225.jpeg)](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/K_PW91.jpeg)\n\nFig.1 K-point convergence for PW91 functional. The Y-axis is the energy difference between successive calculations. The red line represents the convergence criteria.\n\n[![](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/K_PBE-300x225.jpeg)](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/K_PBE.jpeg)\n\nFig. 2 K-point convergence for PBE functional. The Y-axis is the energy difference between successive calculations. The red line represents the convergence criteria.\n\nThe experimentally determined optimal lattice parameter for Fe bcc crystal structure is 2.856 Angstrom [1]. However, it is more appropriate to perform k-point optimization on the lowest lattice dimension being investigated. As the smallest lattice parameter in real space gives the longest parameter in k-space. Hence the K-point optimization is performed at the lattice parameter of 2.3 Angstrom. A very high cut-off energy value is selected here 500 eV to avoid its effects on k-point optimization.\n\nTable 1: K-point grid vs the number of K-points used\n----------------------------------------------------\n\n| K-point grid | K-points used |\n| --- | --- |\n| 3x3x3 | 4 |\n| 4x4x4 | 6 |\n| 5x5x5 | 10 |\n| 6x6x6 | 14 |\n| 7x7x7 | 20 |\n| 8x8x8 | 26 |\n| 9x9x9 | 35 |\n| 10x10x10 | 44 |\n| 12x12x12 | 68 |\n| 15x15x15 | 120 |\n\nFigs.1 and 2 show the absolute change in the consecutive total energy (|ΔE|)\xa0with respect to\xa0the number of irreducible k-points. The corresponding K-point grid is shown in Table 1. As a convergence criterion\xa0of |ΔE| less than 0.04\xa0eV\xa0is used for both the functionals. This corresponds to the optimal grid of 9x9x9.\xa0\n\n### Cut-off energy\n\n[![](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/cut_PW91-300x225.jpeg)](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/cut_PW91.jpeg)\n\nFig. 3 Cut-off energy convergence for PW91 functional. The Y-axis is the energy difference between successive calculations. The red line represents the convergence criteria.\n\n[![](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/cut_PBE-300x225.jpeg)](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/cut_PBE.jpeg)\n\nFig. 4 Cut-off energy convergence for PBE functional. The Y-axis is the energy difference between successive calculations. The red line represents the convergence criteria.\n\nIn Figs. 3 and 4, the absolute change in the consecutive total energy (|ΔE|)\xa0is plotted as a function of\xa0the cut-off energy. As a convergence criterion,\xa0it is\xa0assumed\xa0that the total energy is converged if |ΔE| is less\xa0than 0.03 eV. From Figs. 3 and 4,\xa0it is clear\xa0that the cut-off energy of\xa0400 eV sufficient\xa0considering both the functionals.\xa0A lattice parameter of 2.856 Angstrom and K-point grid of 9x9x9 is used for this convergence.\xa0\n\nFe lattice optimization\n-----------------------\n\nTo optimize the Fe bcc lattice parameter,\xa0the crystal structure energy is calculated for the bcc geometry while changing the lattice parameters from 2.3 to 3.5 angstrom with an interval of 0.1 Angstrom. Based on the K-point and cut off energy convergence study, cut-off energy of 400 eV and a K-point grid of 9x9x9 is employed in these calculations.\xa0\xa0\n\nResults\n=======\n\n[![](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/opt-4-300x225.jpeg)](https://sites.psu.edu/dftap/files/2020/02/opt-4.jpeg)\n\nFig. 5 shows the lattice parameter\xa0optimization. A curve is fitted to the DFT data to get an estimate of the energy minima with respect to the lattice parameter. A polynomial fit of order 4 is used for this purpose for data ranging from lattice parameter 2.6 to 3. Table 2 compares the optimum lattice parameters calculated using both the functionals. Both the functionals give optimum lattice parameter values close to the experimental data. There is hardly any difference between the values determined by both functionals. Table 2 shows the time required for calculations using both the functionals, which is not much different.\xa0\n\nTable 2: Results of calculations\n--------------------------------\n\n| Property | Experimental optimum lattice parameter | DFT optimum lattice parameter (PBE) in Angstrom | DFT optimum lattice parameter (PW91) in Angstrom | Total time for calculations (PBE) in s | Total time for calculations (PW91) in s |\n| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |\n| Value | 2.856 | 2.811 | 2.799 | 61.82 | 59.42 |\n\nConclusions\n===========\n\nDensity Functional Theory-based calculations are used to ascertain the lattice parameters of the\xa0Fe bcc crystal.\xa0It is determined by varying the lattice parameters and calculating their respective energies.\xa0The optimized lattice parameters obtained are reasonably coherent with the experimental results.\xa0A comparative study of the PBE and the PW91 functionals is also performed. It shows that both the functionals give very similar results while using similar computational time although\xa0PBE is a simplification of\xa0PW91 functional [2].\xa0\n\nReferences\n==========\n\n1.
